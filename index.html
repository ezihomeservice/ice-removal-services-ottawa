A driveway can be clear of snow in the afternoon and dangerously slippery by morning. Clearing the snow solves only part of the problem: water can remain on the surface, flow from nearby snowbanks or drain from a roof before freezing again.

For homeowners comparing ice removal services in Ottawa, the useful question is therefore broader than “Who will spread salt?” It is whether the service addresses existing ice, the water feeding it and the conditions that could make it return.

Research from Canadian campuses, winter road trials and local watershed monitoring points toward a practical approach: remove snow early, choose treatments for the conditions, manage meltwater and check the surface again.

Why a Cleared Ottawa Driveway Can Freeze Again

Ice does not require a fresh snowfall. Meltwater moving across a walkway can freeze when temperatures drop. Snow left under vehicle tyres or foot traffic can also become compacted, making later removal more difficult.

Inspect the property for recurring sources of water:

  • Downspouts discharging onto steps or walkways.
  • Snowbanks draining across the driveway.
  • Low spots where water collects.
  • Shaded sections that remain frozen after nearby pavement clears.
  • Runoff crossing the route between the driveway and front door.

The City of Ottawa’s winter salt guidance specifically recommends redirecting downspouts and storing snow where meltwater will not flow onto driveways or walkways.

The implication is straightforward: repeated salting may treat the frozen surface while leaving its source untouched. If the same patch returns after each thaw, investigate the water pathway as well as the ice.

What Research Reveals About Better Ice Control

A Canadian Campus Pilot Used Less Salt With Brine

During winter 2018–2019, Toronto Metropolitan University, then Ryerson University, tested liquid brine at 20 campus locations ahead of snowfall and freezing rain.

The university’s project summary reports that anti-icing with brine required 25% less sodium chloride on average than conventional rock salt. It also reports no increase in complaints or liability during the pilot.

That is useful evidence for preventive treatment, but it is not a promise that every Ottawa property will use 25% less salt. This was a campus project with its own equipment, surfaces and operating conditions. The summary also does not establish a measured reduction in pedestrian injuries.

The practical lesson is to ask whether a provider considers treatment timing, rather than relying exclusively on salt applied after ice has bonded.

Wisconsin Field Trials Support Condition-Based Treatment

A University of Wisconsin–Madison evaluation compared study and control road routes during 143 storms across 10 counties in winter 2020–2021.

Researchers evaluated salt consumption, time to bare or wet pavement, pavement friction and costs. Their findings favoured brine applications: less salt was used, bare or wet conditions were reached sooner, and friction conditions improved. Friction measurements came from two of the participating counties.

These were roadway trials, not residential steps or interlocking driveways. Their relevance is the operating principle: assess treatment through surface conditions and performance, rather than the visible quantity of material spread.

Ottawa’s Streams Show Why Excess Salt Matters

The environmental concern is local. In its 2024–2025 monitoring update, Ottawa Riverkeeper reported that nearly 70% of sampling locations exceeded the chronic chloride guideline of 120 mg/L in early-season monitoring.

That finding describes the monitored locations and sampling period; it does not mean 70% of all Ottawa waterways exceeded the guideline.

The organization’s monitoring program explains that chloride persists in water rather than biodegrading. This makes avoiding unnecessary application worthwhile, even on relatively small properties.

Effective ice management should aim for usable surfaces with an appropriate amount of treatment—not a thick, continuous coating of salt.

Anti-Icing, De-Icing and Grit Serve Different Purposes

These terms are often treated as interchangeable, but they describe different jobs.

MethodMain purposeImportant limitation
Anti-icingTreat a surface before ice bonds, when conditions suit the productTiming and weather affect performance
De-icingHelp loosen or melt ice already on the surfaceProduct, temperature and ice thickness matter
Mechanical removalRemove snow, slush or loosened iceTools must suit the surface
Sand or gritImprove traction on a slippery surfaceIt does not melt the underlying ice

A reasonable treatment plan may combine methods. Removing loose snow first reduces the material left to deal with. Treatment can then target remaining ice, followed by removal of loosened material and another inspection.

Preventive treatment also needs judgment. It should not be understood as a guarantee that a surface will stay ice-free throughout a changing weather event.

Why More Salt May Not Solve the Problem

Road salt lowers water’s freezing point after dissolving into brine. Its practical usefulness depends on conditions, including temperature.

Ottawa’s household guidance advises against applying ordinary road salt below approximately −10°C because it will not provide useful melting under those conditions. Treat this as practical guidance for ordinary road salt, rather than a universal temperature limit for every commercial de-icer.

Before applying a product, check its labelled temperature range, application instructions and suitability for the surface. A product intended for one setting may be unsuitable for another.

If melting is ineffective, adding another layer of the same material may simply leave excess salt behind. Traction material or a different removal approach may be needed.

Avoid assuming that “eco-friendly” means impact-free. Ask what a product contains and why it is appropriate for the property.

What to Ask Ice Removal Services in Ottawa

A useful quote should explain the work being purchased. Snow clearing, salting and follow-up inspections may be separate services.

Does Service Respond to Ice Without Snowfall?

An agreement based only on accumulated snowfall may not address freezing rain or meltwater that freezes overnight. Ask whether an ice-only visit can be requested and how it is priced.

Which Areas Are Included?

Specify the driveway, front steps, side entrance, walkway, bins and any accessible entrance used regularly. Clearing a parking space alone may leave the route to the house slippery.

How Are Treatments Selected?

Ask whether the provider considers surface temperature, material compatibility and existing ice conditions. The answer should explain the process rather than simply promise generous salting.

What Happens When Ice Returns?

Clarify whether a return inspection or treatment is included, separately charged or available only on request. Do not assume a single visit provides ongoing monitoring.

Will the Crew Flag Drainage Problems?

A maintenance crew may identify runoff without being equipped to correct the drainage. Ask how recurring problem areas will be reported so that a longer-term repair can be considered.

A Practical Plan for Recurring Ice

Start by documenting where the problem develops and what happens before it appears. A photograph during a thaw can reveal runoff that is difficult to trace once frozen.

Then build the work around five tasks:

  1. Remove loose snow before traffic compacts it.
  2. Keep snow storage away from drainage routes across walking surfaces.
  3. Match treatment to the conditions and surface.
  4. Remove slush or loosened ice when practical.
  5. Recheck problem areas as temperatures change.

Prioritise the route people actually use, including steps, slopes and entrances. If a section remains hazardous, restrict access where practical until it can be addressed.

Conclusion

Research supports a more deliberate approach to Ottawa ice management: timely clearing, suitable treatments and attention to changing surface conditions. Campus and roadway studies show opportunities to reduce salt use, while local monitoring demonstrates why unnecessary application matters.

When comparing ice removal services in Ottawa, look for a clear plan for existing ice, recurring runoff and follow-up. A freshly treated driveway is only the starting point; the more useful outcome is a route that remains manageable as winter conditions change.

Frequently Asked Questions

Why does ice return after my driveway has been salted?

New meltwater can flow onto a treated area and freeze later. Changing temperatures and treatment conditions can also affect the result. A recurring patch warrants a check of downspouts, snow storage and pooling water.

Does sand remove ice?

No. Sand can improve traction, but it does not melt ice. The underlying frozen surface still needs appropriate management.

Is brine always better than rock salt?

No. Research supports brine in suitable operating conditions, but treatment choice depends on weather, timing, equipment and the surface. Road or campus results cannot guarantee the same outcome at a home.

Is ice control automatically included in snow removal?

Do not assume it is. Ask whether the price includes de-icing, ice-only visits and return treatments after refreezing.
